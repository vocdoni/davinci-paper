An election involves four types of participants: organizer, \keywardens, voters, and sequencers.

\paragraph{Organizer.} The organizer is the entity responsible for defining and setting up the election. Its key responsibilities include defining the voting parameters and gathering the census data. The organizer ensures that the election is structured correctly but does not participate in vote collection or tallying.

\paragraph{\Keywardens.} \Keywardens are a set of decentralized parties that collaboratively generate a public encryption key that voters use to encrypt their votes. The corresponding secret key is secret-shared among them so that only a threshold of key wardens can later collaborate to decrypt the final tally. %Individual ballots remain private because the protocol only invokes threshold decryption on the final aggregate, not on each ballot separately.

\paragraph{Voters.} Voters are the participants that belong the census and are allowed to cast their votes in the election. \\

\paragraph{Sequencers.} Sequencers are a set of parties that during the voting period receive and verify encrypted ballots from voters, reencrypt votes to prevent coercion, and update the shared public state accordingly.
